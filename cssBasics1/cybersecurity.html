What Is Cybersecurity?

Cybersecurity is the practice of protecting computers, networks, programs, and data from attacks, damage, or unauthorized access. Every time you log in to a school account, buy something online, or connect to Wi-Fi, cybersecurity is working in the background to keep your information safe.

Attacks are not just a problem for big companies. Schools, hospitals, small businesses, and everyday people are targeted every day. Learning the basics helps you protect yourself and opens the door to one of the fastest-growing career fields in technology.

The CIA Triad

Security professionals use three core goals, called the CIA Triad, to decide whether information is truly protected:

  • Confidentiality - Only authorized people can see the information. Example: your grades can be viewed by you and your teachers, not by other students.
  • Integrity - Information is accurate and has not been changed by someone who should not change it. Example: no one can secretly edit your bank balance.
  • Availability - Information and systems are there when authorized users need them. Example: the school website stays online during registration.

Common Threats

Attackers use many different tools and tricks. Here are some of the most common:

Threat What It Is Warning Sign
Phishing Fake emails or messages that trick you into clicking a link or sharing a password. Urgent language, misspellings, or a sender address that does not match the company.
Malware Malicious software such as viruses, worms, and spyware. A slow computer, strange pop-ups, or programs you did not install.
Ransomware Malware that locks your files and demands payment to unlock them. Files that will not open and a message demanding money.
Social Engineering Manipulating people, instead of computers, into giving up information. A stranger who calls or messages pretending to be tech support or a teacher.

Warning: No real company or school IT department will ever ask you for your password.

How to Stay Safe

  1. Use long, unique passwords (or passphrases) for every account.
  2. Turn on multi-factor authentication (MFA) whenever it is offered.
  3. Keep your operating system, apps, and browser updated.
  4. Think before you click links or open attachments.
  5. Back up important files so ransomware cannot hold them hostage.

Tip: A passphrase like "Purple-Tractor-Eats-42-Pancakes" is easier to remember and much harder to crack than "P@ssw0rd1".

Careers in Cybersecurity

There are more open cybersecurity jobs than there are trained people to fill them. Here are a few career paths you could explore:

  • Security Analyst - Monitors networks and investigates suspicious activity.
  • Penetration Tester - Legally hacks into systems to find weaknesses before criminals do.
  • Incident Responder - Leads the cleanup and recovery after an attack.
  • Network Administrator - Builds and maintains secure networks for organizations.
